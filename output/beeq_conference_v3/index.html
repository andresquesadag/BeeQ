The problem. Honey bees are important pollinators and are exposed to agricultural active ingredients. The standard for acute toxicity is an in vivo assay that gives the LD₅₀ per bee: reliable, but slow, expensive, and requiring animals.

The third step is what gives the project its purpose: the point is not to take every molecule to the laboratory, but to have a prior comparison telling us which ones are more likely to be toxic and deserve the assay. Prioritise, not replace.

A · ContextResearch question

Does a fixed quantum fidelity kernel induce a predictively and geometrically different similarity structure when the molecular representation, partitions, pre-processing and evaluation are kept unchanged?

Conceptual schematic

BeeQ · IEEE BIP 202604 / 16

The question. This is the paper's question, verbatim. A quantum kernel encodes descriptors into quantum states and uses the fidelity between those states as a similarity measure. That changes how similar two molecules are considered to be, but the classifier can stay classical.

Huang and colleagues showed that the geometric difference between classical and quantum kernels is the key factor for their predictive behaviour to differ. Fidelity kernels can also concentrate exponentially. So the question is empirical.

The pipeline. We start from ApisTox, go through curation and pre-processing, partition by molecular clusters, and then two tracks open: the classical reference models and the three kernels sharing one SVC. Everything converges on the same metrics.

What matters is that everything is fixed except the amber box. That is why any difference can be attributed to the kernel. One caveat: "matched RBF" does not mean matching the bandwidth.

The data. We start from ApisTox, which integrates curated data from ECOTOX, PPDB and BPDB, with 1,035 compounds. After an additional curation and structural standardisation stage — excluding metal-containing structures, handling salts and charge states with RDKit — 893 organic molecules remain: 622 non-toxic and 271 toxic.

Those 893 split into Development, 712 molecules, and a historical holdout of 181. Separately there is CR8, an external panel of eight active ingredients approved in Costa Rica.

The endpoint is binary: toxic if the LD₅₀ is at most 11 micrograms per bee. That threshold comes from ApisTox and the EPA bee-risk guidance, was fixed before modelling, and was not optimised.

If asked: for the 142 excluded molecules a per-operation removal ledger cannot be reconstructed; and the label aggregates the most severe exposure route available, so it is not one homogeneous protocol.

Transition → "Now, how they are split for a fair evaluation."

B · MethodologyPartitions and freezing

Molecular families are never split across sides

BeeQ · IEEE BIP 202606 / 16

The partitions. The central risk in molecular evaluation is similarity leakage: if an evaluation molecule has a near-identical relative in training, the model looks better than it is.

So we cluster first. Morgan fingerprints of radius 2 and 1,024 bits with chirality, Tanimoto similarity, and Butina clustering at a 0.60 cutoff. That gives 462 clusters, 334 of them singletons and the largest with 40 molecules. Whole clusters are assigned disjointly.

At the top you see the composition of the five frozen outer folds and the audit: zero clusters shared across either side of any fold. Note the heterogeneity — fold 4 validates 181 molecules with 59 toxic; fold 2 only 105 with 37. That will explain much of the dispersion we are about to see.

Below, the selection and freezing flow. Within each outer fold's training partition, four inner cluster-grouped splits choose hyperparameters by mean inner AUROC and the threshold by maximum MCC. Then a single refit on all 712, and everything is frozen. The manifest is written before the holdout and CR8 are opened; both are evaluated once.

Caveat: the holdout is "historical" — it was inspected during earlier exploratory stages. And cluster disjointness is not a formal out-of-distribution guarantee.

B · MethodologyThe X10 representation

From a chemical structure to ten numbers

Ethiprole

Structure drawn with RDKit from the frozen CR8 SMILES

·value of the descriptor computed on the structure
zstandardised: (value − mean) / SD, with Development mean and SD
θangle: θ = s · z, what rotates the qubit, with s = 0.25
BeeQ · IEEE BIP 202607 / 16

The representation. Every model receives the same ordered ten-dimensional vector, X10. Here we build it with a concrete molecule, Ethiprole, from the external panel.

Three steps. First, ten physicochemical and structural descriptors are computed on the structure: that is the large value on each card. Second, standardisation: subtract the mean and divide by the standard deviation of the corresponding training partition, never using evaluation data; that is z. Third, and this is what connects to the quantum part: that standardised value is multiplied by the encoding scale s, which is 0.25, giving the angle theta that rotates a qubit.

One descriptor, one qubit. The descriptor order determines which qubit receives which; that is why each card carries its number in the corner. That decision comes back in the future work.

Note that nHal gives the largest angle: Ethiprole has five halogens against a mean of 1.25. LiPHEX gives the most negative one.

The useful analogy is a house fact sheet: floor area, rooms, year built. It lets you compare houses, but nobody would say it reconstructs the house.

C · ImplementationThe idea of a kernel

A kernel is a way of measuring likeness

Instead of asking the model to reason about each molecule on its own, we hand it a table with the likeness of every pair of molecules.

KRBF(z, z′) = exp(−γ ‖z − z′‖²)

The classical reference. It decays with the distance between standardised descriptors; γ controls how fast.

BeeQ · IEEE BIP 202608 / 16

What a kernel is. For those who do not work with this: a kernel is a function that, given two molecules, returns a number saying how similar they are. Do that for every pair and you get a square table, the Gram matrix.

The classical reference is the RBF kernel, which decays exponentially with the squared distance between standardised descriptors. Gamma says how fast it decays, that is, how demanding the notion of "similar" is.

The key point for this talk: the classifier does not change. An SVC receives the pre-computed table and learns the boundary. Changing the kernel changes the similarity geometry, not the machine that decides. That is how we can isolate the kernel's effect.

C · ImplementationQuantum encoding and fidelity

Each descriptor rotates a qubit; likeness is the fidelity

Likeness = fidelity KQ(z, z′) = |⟨ψ(z)|ψ(z′)⟩|² Between 0 and 1; it is 1 when the two states coincide.

Conceptual schematics

  • Exact statevectors in NumPy
  • 1,024 amplitudes per molecule
  • No shots, no noise, no quantum hardware
BeeQ · IEEE BIP 202609 / 16

The quantum part. We take the standardised descriptor and turn it into an angle. On the Bloch sphere, a qubit starting at the |0⟩ pole tilts by an angle theta equal to s times z. With ten descriptors, ten qubits.

The Product map does exactly that: ten independent RY rotations, with no entangling operation. The IQP-ZZ map starts from a uniform superposition with Hadamards, applies a layer of RZ phases and then RZZ phases between neighbouring qubits in a chain, with interaction strength fixed at one and a single repetition. There is no final Hadamard layer.

Note that detail about the chain: only neighbouring qubits are coupled. We will come back to it.

The similarity between two molecules is the fidelity between their states: the squared modulus of the inner product. It lies between zero and one, and equals one when the states coincide.

The scope, worth stating before any result: everything was simulated with exact statevectors in NumPy, 1,024 amplitudes per molecule, without shot sampling, without a noise model and without hardware execution. Qiskit appears in the environment but did not execute the reported campaign. This is not a hardware benchmark.

D · ResultsPerformance on Development

AUROC: ranking, not accuracy Probability that a random toxic molecule scores above a random non-toxic one.

The three kernels landed together Product 0.722 · IQP-ZZ 0.713 · RBF 0.711, with fold-wise SDs of 0.106 to 0.108.

BeeQ · IEEE BIP 202610 / 16

Predictive results. First, what AUROC is, because everything else depends on it: the probability that a randomly chosen toxic molecule scores higher than a randomly chosen non-toxic one. It measures ranking ability, not percentage correct.

Random Forest reached the highest mean, 0.748 ± 0.057. Then logistic regression at 0.733. The three kernels landed very close together: Product 0.722, IQP-ZZ 0.713 and matched RBF 0.711. The balanced MLP, 0.679.

What I want you to see is the dispersion, not the ordering. Each open circle is one fold. The kernels' standard deviations are around 0.106 to 0.108, while the differences between their means are one or two thousandths. Fold 1 drags every kernel below 0.57; fold 5 lifts them above 0.79. Remember the fold composition: they are not interchangeable.

The crosses are the historical holdout, for reference.

Random Forest doing well is consistent with nonlinear relationships within X10; logistic regression being competitive suggests a simple linear model captures much of the signal.

If asked about the paired comparison: the per-fold differences between Product and RBF, and between IQP-ZZ and RBF, average +0.011 and +0.002, do not keep a consistent direction across folds, and change sign on the holdout. The cluster-level paired bootstrap includes zero in all three contrasts.

Transition → "But all of this is ranking ability. What about detection?"

D · ResultsHistorical holdout · n = 181

Ranking well and detecting little are compatible

SI Table S18

Five models hold AUROC near 0.70 and still let most of the toxic compounds through.

BeeQ · IEEE BIP 202611 / 16

The distinction that matters most to a toxicologist. A regulatory decision needs a threshold and a binary answer. And there the picture changes completely.

On the holdout of 181 molecules — 49 toxic and 132 non-toxic — five of the six models hold AUROC between 0.68 and 0.71, similar to Development. The matched RBF even rises to 0.706.

But at the frozen thresholds, sensitivity collapses: between 0.082 and 0.265. The best case is Random Forest, detecting 13 of 49 and letting 36 through. Product and IQP-ZZ detect four each. Specificity is high, between 0.833 and 0.985.

Put differently: these models rank reasonably, but at the chosen operating point they behave as very conservative filters that almost never raise an alarm. For prioritisation that is a serious problem, because the false negative is the costly error.

On the MLP: the one shown is the balanced, retuned variant. It detects 13 of 49 like Random Forest, but at the cost of 22 false positives and an AUROC of 0.482, below chance.

Being inside the domain does not guarantee a correct call

SI Table S27 · balanced MLP

CR8 and the applicability domain. CR8 is an external panel of eight active ingredients approved in Costa Rica under decree 43838, with no overlap against BeeQ893 or ApisTox, verified by canonical SMILES and full InChIKey.

The domain is defined by two criteria derived from Development only. The horizontal axis is structural support: maximum Morgan/Tanimoto similarity to Development, cut at the 5th percentile, 0.223. The vertical axis is descriptor-space support: mean distance to the five nearest neighbours in standardised X10, cut at the 95th percentile, 2.199. Neither was optimised on performance.

Seven of the eight fall inside both domains; the only one outside is Fluoxapiprolin, on descriptor distance.

And here is the point: both toxic compounds, Ethiprole and Isocycloseram, are inside both domains. Ethiprole was called non-toxic by all six models, and Isocycloseram by five: the balanced MLP did detect it, but in exchange produced two false positives, Fluoxapiprolin and Florylpicoxamid. Formal domain membership quantifies proximity to Development; it does not guarantee a correct classification.

With eight compounds and two positives, changing one decision moves sensitivity by half a point. These are compound-level observations, not population-level conclusions. The two axes are weakly associated, Spearman −0.31.

The Ethiprole case. To me this is the most instructive result. Ethiprole's nearest neighbour in Development is fipronil, at Tanimoto similarity 0.740. They are clearly related structures: the same pyrazole core with the dichloro, trifluoromethyl phenyl; what changes is the sulfur substituent.

And fipronil is labelled toxic and was in training. The model had a very similar toxic example and still called Ethiprole non-toxic. All six models failed.

Nor is it a borderline case: Ethiprole's experimental LD₅₀ is well below the 11 µg per bee threshold, so the error is not explained by proximity to the endpoint boundary.

Isocycloseram is the complementary case: its maximum structural support is 0.233, barely above the cutoff. There the problem does look like chemotype extrapolation.

The honest reading is that X10 may not capture some structure-activity relationships relevant to this endpoint. That is a hypothesis for toxicodynamic and biophysical studies, not a conclusion demonstrated here.

E · ConclusionsBounded results

Two findings, and a clear boundary

What we found

  • No consistent predictive improvement Both fidelity kernels landed very close to the matched RBF.
  • No clearly distinct geometry The Gram matrices turned out highly aligned with each other.
  • Detection remains the challenge Beyond Development, sensitivity falls to 0.082–0.265.

What this work does not show

  • It does not demonstrate quantum advantage
  • It does not evaluate noise or real quantum hardware
  • It does not establish clinical or regulatory utility
  • It does not generalise beyond X10, these maps and this protocol
BeeQ · IEEE BIP 202614 / 16

Conclusions. Two findings under molecular-cluster-disjoint evaluation.

First: the quantum kernels evaluated did not deliver a consistent predictive improvement over the matched RBF. They came very close, but without an advantage that persisted across the five folds, and on the holdout the contrasts changed sign.

Second: they did not induce a clearly distinct similarity geometry either. The three kernels' Gram matrices are highly aligned: CKA of 0.982 and 0.985 against the RBF, and 0.9995 between the two quantum kernels.

And a practical finding that goes beyond the quantum question: detection of toxic compounds outside Development is weak.

The right-hand column matters just as much. This does not demonstrate quantum advantage: it shows that these two fixed maps, with this representation and this protocol, do not separate from the RBF. We did not evaluate noise or hardware. It does not establish clinical or regulatory utility. And it does not generalise beyond X10 and these maps.

E · ConclusionsFuture work

Separating kernel changes from representation changes

  • Optimise the encoding scale Bandwidth turned out to be decisive; it deserves to be an object of study rather than a by-product of the search.
  • Train the embedding by kernel-target alignment Instead of fixed maps, learn the map's parameters.
  • Rearrange and widen the qubits Duplicate qubits per descriptor, add more descriptors, or change the order: since the RZZ chain only couples neighbours, which descriptor sits next to which may matter.

SI Table S5 · descriptive

BeeQ · IEEE BIP 202615 / 16

Future work. Three concrete directions.

First: extend encoding-scale optimisation. Bandwidth turned out to be the decisive factor, so it deserves to be treated as an object of study rather than a by-product of the hyperparameter search.

Second: instead of fixed maps, train the embedding parameters through kernel-target alignment.

Third, and this is where the figure comes in: rearrange and widen the qubits. Recall that in the IQP-ZZ map the RZZ interactions only couple neighbouring qubits in a chain. That means which descriptor sits next to which determines which pairs of features can interact. Today the order is simply inherited from the table.

The figure shows how much signal each descriptor carries on its own in Development. nOP is the most informative at 0.675; LiPHEX alone is close to chance. One line of work is to use that structure to decide the qubit ordering, duplicate qubits per descriptor to retain more context, or add descriptors. I stress that this figure is descriptive: no descriptor was selected or removed from it.
