\begin{abstract}
Species-specific pesticide toxicity data are limited, which motivates compact
and reproducible screening models. We evaluate ten physicochemical and
structural molecular descriptors (X10) for binary acute toxicity to the honey
bee, \textit{Apis mellifera}. The curated domain contains 893 molecules, with
712 development compounds assigned to five frozen structure-aware folds and a
cluster-disjoint 181-molecule historical holdout. Class-weighted logistic
regression, random forest, and radial basis function (RBF) support vector
machines are compared with exact-statevector product and entangled IQP-ZZ
fidelity kernels on identical descriptors and molecules. Random forest gives
the highest development ranking performance (AUROC 0.756, 95\% bootstrap
interval 0.714--0.793), while the matched RBF reaches 0.710 on the historical
holdout. Removing the explicit organophosphorus count lowers development
AUROC by 0.048 for logistic regression and 0.052 for random forest. Removing
both partition descriptors lowers RBF AUROC by 0.047. The IQP-ZZ kernel
improves development AUROC over matched RBF by 0.018 (0.004--0.032), but not
historical-holdout AUROC (-0.006, -0.026--0.015). Quantum and RBF kernels are
strongly aligned and disagree on few classifications. X10 therefore contains
useful screening signal, but the experiment does not establish a generalizable
quantum advantage.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Honey bees are economically and ecologically important pollinators, yet
species-specific toxicity measurements cover only a small fraction of the
chemical space relevant to agriculture. Public ecotoxicology resources are
large but require structural standardization, deduplication, endpoint
selection, and label harmonization before supervised learning
\cite{olker2022ecotox}. ApisTox addressed part of this gap by curating a
benchmark for pesticide and agrochemical toxicity to \textit{Apis mellifera}
\cite{adamczyk2025apistox}.

Recent evaluation on ApisTox found that molecular fingerprints and graph
kernels can outperform more complex graph neural and pretrained molecular
models \cite{adamczyk2026evaluating}. This result motivates a complementary
question: how much predictive information is retained by a much smaller,
interpretable descriptor panel? A compact representation is attractive for
auditing, controlled ablation, and near-term quantum kernel experiments, where
qubit and simulation costs grow with input dimension.

This work contributes: 1) a leakage-controlled evaluation of ten compact
descriptors under frozen, cluster-disjoint folds; 2) pre-specified ablations of
an organophosphorus motif and two partition descriptors; 3) a matched
classical-quantum comparison using exact fidelity kernels; and 4) a
hash-addressed workflow connecting data, predictions, figures, and the
manuscript. The goal is not to claim quantum advantage, but to test whether a
quantum feature map induces behavior that is detectably different from an RBF
kernel under equal molecular coordinates.

\section{Background}

\subsection{Bee Toxicity Prediction}

ApisTox contains standardized structures, toxicity labels, and challenging
predefined splits \cite{adamczyk2025apistox}. Fingerprints such as ECFP encode
local molecular neighborhoods \cite{rogers2010ecfp}, while graph kernels
compare molecular topology directly \cite{vishwanathan2010graphkernels}.
Both are strong references for molecular property prediction. The present
study instead uses a curated subset for which ten conventional and in-house
descriptors are finite and available. It is therefore a targeted follow-up,
not a reproduction of the full ApisTox benchmark.

\subsection{Quantum Kernels}

Quantum feature maps encode classical inputs into states in a Hilbert space;
state overlap then defines a kernel for a conventional classifier
\cite{havlicek2019supervised,schuld2021kernel}. This formulation permits a
controlled comparison because the optimization after kernel construction is
the same support vector machine used classically. It also requires caution:
different kernels can be classically simulated, and favorable validation
performance alone is not evidence of computational advantage.

\section{Materials and Methods}

\subsection{Curated Domain and Endpoint}

The binary endpoint uses the strongest available acute LD50 per molecule:
toxic when LD50 $\leq 11\,\mu$g/bee and non-toxic otherwise. The curated domain
contains organic, metal-free molecules with finite X10 values. Development and
holdout labels are moderately imbalanced (Table~\ref{tab:data}). The holdout
was inspected during descriptor pre-screening before this study; we therefore
call it a \emph{historical holdout}, not an untouched confirmatory test.

The split was constructed using 1024-bit Morgan fingerprints with radius two,
chirality enabled, Butina clustering at cutoff 0.60
\cite{butina1999unsupervised}, and group-aware stratification. Development and
holdout share no Butina cluster. Each development cluster belongs to exactly
one of five frozen folds.

\subsection{X10 Representation and Hypotheses}

X10 includes molecular weight (MolWt), lipophilicity (MolLogP), topological
polar surface area with sulfur and phosphorus contributions (TPSA), hydrogen
bond donors, rotatable bonds, aromatic rings, halogen count, an explicit
organophosphorus motif count ($n_{OP}$), an in-house hexadecane/water partition
estimate (LiPHEX), and an in-house polar solvent-accessible surface fraction.
The fixed feature order is part of the run hash.

We test five hypotheses: X10 separates the toxicity classes; $n_{OP}$ adds
signal beyond global physicochemical variables; MolLogP and LiPHEX contribute
complementary partition information; quantum and RBF kernels differ under
matched coordinates; and predictive ranking persists on cluster-disjoint
molecules. Ablations remove $n_{OP}$, MolLogP, LiPHEX, or both partition
descriptors without changing any other protocol component.

\subsection{Classical Models}

The classical models are class-weighted logistic regression, RBF-SVC, and a
300-tree random forest. Standardization is fitted only on rows available to a
training partition. Each frozen fold serves once as outer validation. Within
each outer-training partition, four-fold stratified group cross-validation,
grouped by Butina cluster, selects hyperparameters by AUROC. Logistic $C$ is
chosen from $\{0.01,0.1,1,10,100\}$; RBF uses
$C\in\{0.1,1,10,100\}$ and
$\gamma\in\{0.01,0.03,0.1,0.3,\text{scale}\}$. Random forest depth and minimum
leaf size are selected from a small predeclared grid. The five outer
predictions are pooled so that each development molecule contributes exactly
one out-of-fold (OOF) score.

\subsection{Matched Simulated Quantum Kernels}

One standardized descriptor is assigned to each of ten qubits. The product
control map is
\begin{equation}
 |\phi_P(\mathbf{x})\rangle=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{10}
 R_y(sx_j)|0\rangle .
\end{equation}
The entangled IQP-ZZ map starts in a uniform superposition and assigns basis
state $\mathbf{z}\in\{-1,1\}^{10}$ the phase
\begin{equation}
 \exp\!\left[i\left(s\sum_j x_jz_j +
 \eta s^2\sum_{j=1}^{9}x_jx_{j+1}z_jz_{j+1}\right)\right],
\end{equation}
with $\eta=1$. Exact complex statevectors define
$K_Q(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}')=|\langle\phi(\mathbf{x})|
\phi(\mathbf{x}')\rangle|^2$. No sampling noise or hardware backend is used.

The angle scale $s\in\{0.125,0.25,0.5,1\}$ and
$C\in\{0.1,1,10,100\}$ are selected using the same nested folds and criterion
as RBF. An RBF kernel with four $\gamma$ values and the same four $C$ values is
recomputed in the quantum pipeline, giving each kernel family a 16-point
search budget. Every Gram matrix is checked for finiteness, symmetry, unit
diagonal, and positive semidefiniteness.

\subsection{Metrics and Statistical Analysis}

AUROC is primary; AUPRC, balanced accuracy, and Matthews correlation
coefficient (MCC) are secondary. We use 2,000 class-stratified bootstrap
resamples for 95\% intervals. Model and ablation differences use paired
resampling of molecule IDs. Kernel geometry is characterized by centered
alignment, target alignment, and entropy-based effective rank. To probe
domain shift, holdout molecules are divided into tertiles by nearest Euclidean
distance in standardized X10 space. All random seeds, folds, configurations,
predictions, and output files are recorded with SHA-256 hashes.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Compact Descriptors Carry Predictive Signal}

All X10 models rank toxic molecules above chance in development and in the
historical holdout (Table~\ref{tab:main-results}). Random forest is strongest
in development at AUROC 0.756, while the matched RBF is strongest on the
historical holdout at 0.710. The larger holdout intervals reflect its smaller
sample size (181 molecules, 49 positive). Threshold metrics do not follow the
same ordering: RBF has the highest development MCC (0.466), whereas logistic
regression has the highest X10 holdout MCC (0.398). This supports the X10
signal hypothesis while showing that ranking and fixed-threshold behavior
must be reported separately.

\subsection{Structural and Partition Ablations}

Removing $n_{OP}$ decreases development AUROC by 0.048 for logistic
regression (95\% paired interval 0.021--0.073) and 0.052 for random forest
(0.028--0.076), but only 0.002 for RBF (-0.028--0.031). Thus the explicit
motif adds measurable signal, although its effect depends on model family.
Only 12 holdout molecules have $n_{OP}>0$, so motif-specific holdout estimates
are too sparse for a stable conclusion.

MolLogP and LiPHEX have Spearman correlation 0.405 in development. For RBF,
removing MolLogP lowers AUROC by 0.024 (0.004--0.044), removing LiPHEX lowers
it by 0.031 (0.002--0.062), and removing both lowers it by 0.047
(0.013--0.079). This supports complementary partition information for the RBF
geometry. LiPHEX removal changes logistic and random-forest AUROC by only
-0.003 and -0.004, respectively, with intervals crossing zero
(Table~\ref{tab:ablation}).

\subsection{Classical Versus Quantum Kernels}

In development, the product and IQP-ZZ kernels reach AUROC 0.729 and 0.732,
respectively, compared with 0.714 for matched RBF. Paired improvements are
0.015 (0.001--0.030) and 0.018 (0.004--0.032). These gains do not transfer to
the historical holdout: product minus RBF is -0.007
(-0.038--0.024), and IQP-ZZ minus RBF is -0.006 (-0.026--0.015).

The kernels are also less behaviorally distinct than their names suggest.
Across outer-training folds, centered alignment with RBF is 0.989 for the
product map and 0.992 for IQP-ZZ (Table~\ref{tab:geometry}). Their OOF class
decisions differ from RBF on only 1.4\% and 1.1\% of development molecules;
holdout disagreement is 1.7\% and 2.8\%. IQP-ZZ has greater effective rank
than RBF but remains strongly aligned. The experiment therefore detects a
small validation ranking difference, not a robust quantum advantage.

\subsection{Structure-Disjoint Generalization}

X10 AUROC remains between 0.689 and 0.710 on the historical holdout, providing
evidence that ranking signal persists across disjoint Butina clusters. The
signal weakens with descriptor-space distance: near-tertile AUROC is 0.829 for
random forest and 0.760--0.776 for the three kernel models, whereas far-tertile
AUROC falls to 0.601--0.642. Error rates rise from 0.15--0.20 in the near
tertile to 0.32--0.35 in the far tertile (Fig.~\ref{fig:distance}). This
pattern identifies an applicability limitation shared by classical and
quantum methods.

\section{Discussion}

The results support the principal claim that a ten-descriptor representation
contains useful acute bee-toxicity signal under structure-aware evaluation.
The strongest development model is not the strongest holdout model, and
model-specific ablations matter: $n_{OP}$ is important for linear and tree
models, while both partition descriptors jointly shape the RBF kernel. These
findings favor retaining X10 as the common comparison space rather than
selecting a smaller panel from the historical holdout.

The quantum comparison is intentionally conservative. Both maps use the same
standardized coordinates, molecules, folds, class weights, and search budget
as RBF. Exact statevectors remove shot noise and backend drift, isolating the
feature-map question. IQP-ZZ yields a statistically positive OOF AUROC
difference, but the difference reverses slightly and becomes uncertain on the
holdout. High kernel alignment and low decision disagreement further indicate
that the tested quantum maps mostly reproduce an RBF-like neighborhood
structure. This is useful negative evidence: a different circuit construction
or a representation containing richer structural coordinates would be needed
to test genuinely distinct geometry.

Several limitations bound the claims. First, the historical holdout was
consulted during earlier descriptor pre-screening and cannot support a
confirmatory claim. Second, X10 is available for 893 curated molecules rather
than the full ApisTox collection, and two in-house descriptors are not yet
recomputable from public code. Third, the endpoint collapses exposure routes
into one thresholded label. Fourth, exact ten-qubit simulation demonstrates a
quantum kernel method, not computational speedup or hardware robustness.
Finally, the distance analysis uses standardized descriptors; Butina cluster
separation supplies the structural control, but a future regional set should
also report fingerprint similarity and independent toxicity provenance.

\section{Reproducibility}

The workflow is split into data audit, classical models, quantum kernels,
statistical analysis, and manuscript generation. Source CSVs remain outside
version control. A canonical split hash fixes molecule IDs, labels, clusters,
and folds. Each run stores package versions, Git state, configurations,
per-fold parameters, OOF and holdout predictions, metrics, and output hashes.
The notebook loads committed result bundles when expensive experiments are not
recomputed and also provides explicit execution cells for every phase.

\section{Conclusion}

Ten compact descriptors distinguish toxic from non-toxic pesticides under
cluster-disjoint evaluation. Explicit organophosphorus structure and two
partition descriptors contribute model-dependent information. Simulated
quantum kernels produce small OOF ranking gains and measurably different
spectra, but their strong alignment with RBF and lack of holdout improvement
do not support a generalizable quantum advantage. The resulting reproducible
baseline is suitable for future circuit studies and independently labeled
regional validation.
